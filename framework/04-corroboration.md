# 4. Corroboration

**The question:** does anyone other than you say it?

This is the dimension brands score worst on, and the slowest to fix. A model deciding whether to name a brand weighs what independent sources say, not just what the brand says about itself. A well-structured site with no outside mentions often loses to a messier competitor that shows up in reviews, directories, comparisons and forum threads.

## Checklist (0–20)

| Check | Points | Pass condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Third-party mentions in the category | 6 | The brand is named in independent articles, comparisons or lists for its category, not only for its own name. |
| Review and directory presence | 4 | Listed with consistent facts on the review sites and directories the category uses (G2, Clutch, Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, industry-specific ones). |
| Community presence | 4 | Real mentions in Reddit, Quora, forums or community Q&A where buyers ask for recommendations. |
| Claims backed externally | 3 | The brand's headline claims (awards, client counts, results) can be found stated by someone else. |
| Original data others reuse | 3 | The brand has published data, research or tools that other sites link to or quote. |

## How to test

- Search for the category query without the brand name ("best [category] in [market]") and count how many results mention the brand.
- Ask the engines the same query and list every source they cite. Those domains are where corroboration needs to exist.
- Take each headline claim on the brand's site and search for it. Unsupported claims are a liability.

## What moves this

- Original research and data that journalists and bloggers can cite.
- Getting listed where the engines already look for your category, which you learn from step two above.
- Answering real questions in communities with genuinely useful answers, disclosed as the brand.

Paid links and fake reviews do not move this in any lasting way and create risk. Engines are trained on the same spam signals search engines are.
