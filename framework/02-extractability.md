# 2. Extractability

**The question:** does a claim still make sense after it is lifted off the page?

Generative engines retrieve passages, not pages. A retrieved chunk might be three sentences with no headline, no navigation and no surrounding paragraphs. If your key claim only makes sense in context ("As mentioned above, we cut it by 40%"), the model cannot use it.

## Checklist (0–20)

| Check | Points | Pass condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Self-contained claims | 5 | Key claims name their subject and give their number in the same sentence ("Acme reduced invoice processing time by 40% for 12 logistics clients in 2025"). |
| Answer-first sections | 4 | Each section opens with the direct answer to its heading, followed by the detail. |
| Specifics over adjectives | 4 | Numbers, dates, names, prices and conditions instead of "leading", "fast", "affordable". |
| Question-shaped headings | 3 | Headings match the questions buyers ask ("How much does X cost?") where that reads naturally. |
| Lists and tables for comparisons | 2 | Comparisons, steps and specs are in HTML lists or tables, not paragraphs or images. |
| Dated content | 2 | Visible publish and update dates on pages that make time-sensitive claims. |

## How to test

- Take any paragraph and paste it on its own into a blank document. Can a stranger tell who it is about and what it claims?
- Count the claims on a key page that contain a number, a name or a date. Pages that get cited usually have many.
- Search the page for pronouns that start a section ("It", "This", "They") and replace them with the noun.

## Common failures

- Proof locked in images, carousels, PDFs or video with no text equivalent.
- A homepage that states a positioning line but no verifiable facts.
- FAQs that answer with "Contact us to find out".
